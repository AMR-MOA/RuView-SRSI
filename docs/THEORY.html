Part 0 — Orientation

0.1 The ninety-second version

Every WiFi device in your home is already flooding your rooms with 2.4 GHz radio waves. Those waves bounce off walls, furniture, and — crucially — off you. The receiver has to measure exactly how the room distorted the signal, otherwise it could not decode the data. That measurement is called Channel State Information (CSI), and it is computed by essentially every WiFi chip on Earth, several hundred times a second, and then thrown away.

RuView does not throw it away.

   ┌──────────┐        radio waves        ┌──────────┐
   │  WiFi TX │ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~► │  ESP32-S3│
   │  (any AP)│    ╲                  ╱   │  receiver│
   └──────────┘     ╲   ┌────────┐   ╱    └────┬─────┘
                     ╲─►│ person │─►╱          │ CSI: 52–64 complex
                        └────────┘             │ numbers, ~20×/second
                     reflection changes        ▼
                     with every breath    ┌─────────────┐
                                          │  DSP + tiny │
                                          │   models    │
                                          └──────┬──────┘
                                                 ▼
                       presence · breathing rate · heart rate ·
                       motion · falls · posture · (aspirationally) pose

The whole project is the engineering required to go from "the radio field is perturbed" to "a person is lying down in the bedroom, breathing at 14 breaths per minute, and has not moved for six hours" — without a camera, without a wearable, in the dark, and through a drywall wall.

0.2 What is physically true, and what is engineering

Two claims are worth separating immediately, because the field is full of hype.

True, and not controversial: a human body is a large, wet, strongly reflecting object at 2.4 GHz. Moving it — even by the few millimetres your chest moves when you breathe — measurably changes the multipath channel. This is not speculative; it is the same physics that makes radar work, and it has been demonstrated in peer-reviewed work since roughly 2013. [PHYS]

Hard, and where all the real work is: the channel is also perturbed by the fan, the microwave, the neighbour's access point changing power, the temperature of the room, and your own hardware's clock drift. Extracting "person" from that soup, in a room the system has never seen before, is the actual problem. Most of this repository is about that problem, not about the physics.

Part 1 — Motivation: why build this at all

1.1 The problem: rooms are unobserved

There is a large and growing class of questions that are trivially answerable if you are physically present, and nearly impossible to answer otherwise:

  • Did my father get out of bed this morning?
  • Is the person in the bathroom still conscious?
  • How many people are in this conference room right now?
  • Did the resident's breathing pattern change over the last three weeks?
  • Is anyone still alive under this collapsed building?

Every existing answer has a serious cost.

ApproachWhat it costs
CamerasTotal privacy loss. Socially unacceptable in bedrooms and bathrooms — which is exactly where falls happen. Fails in darkness and behind furniture. Legally fraught (GDPR, HIPAA, state biometric laws).
WearablesOnly work if worn. Compliance among elderly users is famously poor. Must be charged. The person most likely to fall is the least likely to be wearing the pendant.
PIR motion sensorsDetect gross motion only. A person who has collapsed is, to a PIR sensor, indistinguishable from an empty room. No vitals, no posture, no count.
Pressure mats / bed sensorsSingle-point, easily defeated by walking around them.
mmWave radarGenuinely good (see Part 7) — but a dedicated 60 GHz sensor costs $30–200 per room, does not penetrate walls, and requires new infrastructure in every room you want covered.

1.2 The opportunity: the infrastructure is already installed

The argument for WiFi sensing is not that it is the most sensitive modality. It is not. The argument is marginal cost:

  • Radio infrastructure already exists in essentially every occupied building.
  • The measurement (CSI) is already computed as a mandatory part of decoding a packet. Sensing is a free by-product of a link that is running anyway.
  • 2.4 GHz penetrates interior walls. One sensor can cover more than one room.
  • It works in complete darkness, through smoke, and under blankets.
  • A capable node costs $6–10 (ESP32-C6 / ESP32-S3), not $200.

That last number is what makes the design space different. At $9/node you can afford to put six nodes in a room and solve with geometric diversity what a single expensive sensor would have to solve with raw sensitivity. This is the central architectural bet of this project, and it is what "multistatic" means in practice (see §2.11).

1.4 The privacy argument — and its limits

The honest framing is that WiFi sensing is **privacy-preferable, not privacy-free**.

What it genuinely improves: there is no image. A leaked CSI recording does not show a person's face, body, or what they were reading. The failure mode of a compromised camera and the failure mode of a compromised CSI stream are not comparable.

What it does not solve: CSI is still a biometric-adjacent signal. Published research has shown gait can be used to identify individuals from RF, and this repository takes that seriously rather than waving it away — see the BFLD privacy class system (ADR-120), where privacy classes P0–P5 gate what may leave the device at all, and raw and identity-bearing tiers are structurally forbidden from egress (v2/crates/wifi-densepose-bfld/). The architecture's answer to "this could be abused" is edge-first computation: the raw signal never leaves the box, only the derived semantic state does.

That is a design stance, not a proof. It should be evaluated as such.

1.5 Why now

Three things changed roughly simultaneously:

  1. Cheap CSI-capable silicon. Espressif exposed the CSI callback on the ESP32 family. Before that, CSI extraction required an Intel 5300 NIC with a patched driver on a specific kernel, or a jailbroken Broadcom chip.
  2. The IEEE standardised sensing. IEEE 802.11bf defines WLAN sensing as a first-class service — sensing sessions, measurement negotiation, and reporting. This turns "clever hack" into "protocol feature", and every major chipset vendor now has a sensing story.
  3. Small models became viable on microcontrollers. A 8 KB int4 embedding model that runs in microseconds changes what "edge" means.

Part 2 — Physics from first principles

This part builds the physical model with no assumed background. If you already know OFDM and multipath, skip to §2.6.

2.1 Radio waves, in the only terms you need

An electromagnetic wave is an oscillating electric and magnetic field that propagates through space at c ≈ 3 × 108 m/s. Two properties matter here:

  • Frequency f — oscillations per second. WiFi's 2.4 GHz band means 2.4 × 109 oscillations per second.
  • Wavelength λ — the physical distance between successive wave crests, λ = c/f.
Physics

The single most important number in this document

λ = cf = 3 × 108 m/s2.4 × 109 Hz = 0.125 m = 125 mm

At 5 GHz, λ = 60 mm.

Every sensitivity claim in this project traces back to this. A radio wave at 2.4 GHz completes a full cycle every 12.5 cm of travel. Therefore a path-length change of a few millimetres is a large fraction of a cycle — and phase is measurable to a fraction of a degree.

2.2 What happens to a wave in a room

A transmitted wave does not reach the receiver by one path. It:

  • Attenuates with distance (free-space path loss),
  • Reflects off conductive and high-permittivity surfaces (walls, metal, wet tissue),
  • Diffracts around edges,
  • Scatters off objects comparable in size to λ,
  • Penetrates materials with loss.
Physics

Free-space path loss

FSPL(dB) = 20log10(d) + 20log10(f) - 147.55

At d = 1 m and f = 2.4 GHz: 0 + 187.6 - 147.55 ≈ 40 dB. Doubling the distance costs another 6 dB. This is why node placement dominates results — the difference between 1 m and 4 m from a subject is 12 dB of link budget, which is most of your breathing SNR.

Building-material penetration at 2.4 GHz, one-way, approximately:

MaterialLossConsequence for this project
Drywall / plasterboard3–5 dBThrough-wall sensing works well
Wooden door3–6 dBWorks
Brick8–15 dBMarginal; needs close nodes
Concrete15–25 dBDifficult
Reinforced concrete / metal mesh25 dB+Effectively opaque

This table is why the repo bounds its through-wall claim at "up to ~5 m, signal-dependent" rather than a flat number. It is also why the test plan's through-wall step tells you to start with drywall.

2.3 Multipath: the room as a filter

Because there are many paths, the receiver sees the sum of many copies of the transmitted signal, each with its own delay and attenuation. Mathematically, the room is a linear filter with impulse response

h(τ) = ∑p ap  δ(τ - τp)

where path p arrives with complex gain ap after delay τp. This h(τ) is the Channel Impulse Response (CIR) — literally "what the room does to an instantaneous pulse".

Its Fourier transform, H(f), is the Channel Frequency Response (CFR) — what the room does to each frequency. These are two views of the same object:

   CIR  h(τ)   — time domain            CFR  H(f)  — frequency domain
   ┌──────────────────────┐             ┌──────────────────────┐
   │ █                    │             │  ▁▃▅▂▇▁▄▆▂▁▅▃▁▂▄     │
   │ █  █                 │   ◄─ FFT ─► │                      │
   │ █  █    █     █      │             │                      │
   └──────────────────────┘             └──────────────────────┘
    direct  wall  person  far            per-subcarrier gain/phase
    path    bounce        bounce

This equivalence is load-bearing in this codebase. WiFi hands you H(f) (that is what CSI is). Physical intuition lives in h(τ) — "how far away is the thing that reflected". Converting one to the other is exactly what ruvsense/cir.rs does, implementing ADR-134.

2.4 OFDM: why WiFi gives you many measurements, not one

WiFi does not transmit one carrier. Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiplexing (OFDM) splits the channel into many narrow, mutually orthogonal subcarriers transmitted in parallel.

For 802.11n on a 20 MHz channel:

QuantityValueDerivation
FFT size64Standard
Subcarrier spacing312.5 kHz20 MHz / 64
Non-null subcarriers5652 data + 4 pilot; the rest are guard/DC nulls
Symbol duration3.2 µs + 0.8 µs guard1 / 312.5 kHz

Why OFDM matters for sensing rather than communication: to equalise each subcarrier, the receiver must estimate the channel at each subcarrier separately. Instead of one number describing the room, you get 56 — a sampled version of H(f) across 20 MHz of bandwidth. That is a spectrum, and spectra carry structure.

Note

Note on subcarrier counts you will see in this repo. The ESP32 CSI callback returns raw buffers whose length depends on the PHY mode: HT20 yields 64 entries (of which the guard bands are null), HT40 yields 128. Different chipsets report differently — Intel 5300 gives 30 grouped subcarriers, Atheros gives 56, Broadcom via Nexmon gives the full FFT. The repo normalises all of these onto a canonical 56-bin grid in hardware_norm.rs (ADR-027), using Catmull-Rom cubic interpolation, precisely so a model trained on one chipset is not useless on another. [CODE]

2.5 CSI: the actual measurement

For each subcarrier k, the receiver estimates a complex number:

Hk = |Hk|   ej∠ Hk

  • |Hk| — amplitude: how much that frequency was attenuated.
  • ∠ Hk — phase: how much that frequency was delayed, modulo 2π.

Stack those across k and you have one CSI frame. Stack frames over time and you have a 2-D signal: subcarrier × time.

             subcarrier index k →
          ┌──────────────────────────────────┐
    t=0   │ 3.1  2.8  4.0  3.9  2.2  ...     │  amplitudes
    t=1   │ 3.1  2.7  4.1  3.8  2.3  ...     │
    t=2   │ 3.0  2.9  3.9  4.0  2.2  ...     │   ← a person breathing
     ↓    │  ⋮                               │     shows up as a slow
   time   └──────────────────────────────────┘     ripple down a column

Everything downstream in this project is a function of this matrix.

In this repo, one frame is CsiFrame in wifi-densepose-core, and it is the type every other crate depends on.

2.6 Why a human body shows up at all

At 2.4 GHz, biological tissue is mostly water, and water has a very high relative permittivity. Approximate values at 2.45 GHz:

MediumRelative permittivity εrConductivity σ (S/m)
Air1.00
Dry skin~38~1.5
Muscle~53~1.7
Pure water~78—
Physics

Reflection at the air–body boundary

For normal incidence, the reflection coefficient is Γ = √ε1-√ε2√ε1+√ε2 With ε1 = 1 (air) and ε2 ≈ 53 (muscle): |Γ| = |1 - 7.28|1 + 7.28 ≈ 0.76    ⇒    |Γ|2 ≈ 0.58

Roughly 58% of incident power is reflected at the body surface. A human is not a weak scatterer — a human is close to a mirror. Additionally, the penetration depth in muscle at 2.45 GHz is only ~2 cm, so the body is effectively an opaque, strongly-reflecting obstacle, not a transparent one.

This is the physical basis of the entire field. It is also why the signal is obstructed by the body — a node behind you sees a shadow, and a node in front of you sees a reflection. Multistatic geometry exploits exactly this asymmetry.

2.7 Breathing: from millimetres to degrees

Chest-wall displacement during normal quiet breathing is 4–12 mm. Here is why that is enormous.

Physics

Phase sensitivity to displacement

If a reflecting surface moves by Δ d along the propagation direction, the reflected path length changes. For a monostatic geometry (transmitter and receiver co-located) the path changes by 2Δ d and Δφ = 2π · 2Δ dλ For a bistatic geometry the factor is between 1 and 2 depending on the angle. Taking the conservative one-way form used in the repo's test plan: Δφ = 2π Δ dλ = 2π × 0.0120.125 = 0.60 rad ≈ 35°

A 12 mm chest movement produces roughly 35° of phase shift (and up to ~70° in the monostatic limit). Phase noise on a clean link is a small number of degrees. The breathing signal is therefore tens of dB above the noise floor — in principle.

The "in principle" carries all the weight. It assumes the chest motion is aligned with the propagation path, the person is in a favourable Fresnel zone, nothing else in the room is moving, and hardware phase artefacts have been removed. §2.9 explains why that last assumption is the difficult one.

Heartbeat, for comparison, produces surface displacement on the order of 0.2–0.5 mm — 20–50× smaller, hence ~26–34 dB weaker, and it sits underneath the harmonics of breathing. This is the honest reason heart rate is the weakest capability in this system, and why the test plan tells you to hold your breath when measuring it.

2.9 The hardware lies: CFO, SFO, and why raw phase is garbage

Here is the fact that surprises everyone new to CSI: the phase you read out of a commodity WiFi chip is not the physical channel phase. It is the physical phase plus a pile of hardware artefacts that are often larger than the signal.

ArtefactCauseEffect on measured phase
Carrier Frequency Offset (CFO)TX and RX local oscillators are not identicalAdds a term that drifts packet to packet
Sampling Frequency Offset (SFO)ADC clocks differAdds a term linear in subcarrier index
Packet Detection Delay (PDD)Symbol timing estimate jittersAlso linear in subcarrier index, random per packet
Phase-Locked Loop noiseOscillator instabilityRandom walk
Automatic Gain Control (AGC)RX gain changes per packetAmplitude scale jumps discontinuously

Consequence: raw per-packet phase is effectively random. Any pipeline that uses it naively produces noise and calls it a signal.

There are three standard remedies, and this repo uses all three where applicable:

  1. Linear detrending / phase sanitization. SFO and PDD are both linear in subcarrier index, so unwrap the phase across subcarriers and subtract the best-fit line. This removes the linear artefacts at the cost of destroying absolute time-of-flight. Implemented in phase_sanitizer.rs. [CODE]
  1. Conjugate multiplication (the CSI-ratio model). If you have two antennas sharing one RF chain, they share the same CFO/SFO/PDD. Computing Hi[k] · Hj[k] cancels all common-mode terms and leaves only the environmental difference. This is the technique from SpotFi (SIGCOMM 2015) and IndoTrack (MobiCom 2017), implemented in csi_ratio.rs. [CODE]
Note

Important limitation for this hardware. The ESP32-S3 is single antenna — the ADR-018 frame header carries n_ant = 1. Conjugate multiplication across antennas is therefore unavailable on the primary node hardware. That path exists for multi-antenna sources (Intel 5300, Atheros, MediaTek MIMO). On ESP32 the system compensates with amplitude-based features, sanitized phase, and multiple nodes instead of multiple antennas. This is a real and under-appreciated architectural consequence.

  1. Differential / baseline-referenced measurement. Instead of asking "what is the phase", ask "how has the phase changed relative to the empty-room baseline". Artefacts that are stationary over the measurement window cancel. This is the entire basis of ADR-135 and the calibrate command.

2.10 Fresnel zones: why placement dominates everything

Between any transmitter and receiver there is a family of nested ellipsoids — Fresnel zones — defined by path-length difference. A reflector inside the first Fresnel zone contributes to the received signal roughly in phase with the direct path; one in the second contributes out of phase, and so on.

Physics

First Fresnel zone radius

rn = √n λ d1 d2d1 + d2

For a 5 m link with the subject at the midpoint (d1 = d2 = 2.5 m), n=1, λ = 0.125 m: r1 = √1 × 0.125 × 2.5 × 2.55 = √0.156 ≈ 0.40 m

The practical consequence is severe and counter-intuitive: as a chest moves, it crosses Fresnel boundaries, and the received amplitude oscillates. At some positions breathing produces a strong sinusoid; move the person 3 cm and the same breathing produces almost nothing (a "blind spot"), or produces a doubled apparent rate as the chest crosses a boundary twice per breath.

This is modelled in fresnel.rs and studied in examples/research-sota/01-physics-floor/.

It is the reason the repo's placement study reports a 93× sensing-coverage difference between naive and physics-aware placement [SYNTH], and why the test plan puts node placement (Step 2.4) before any hardware is powered on. Placement is not a detail. Placement is the dominant term.

2.11 Multistatic geometry: why more nodes beats a better node

A single TX–RX pair gives you one projection of the scene. It cannot distinguish "a small motion nearby" from "a large motion far away", and it has blind zones where the geometry is unfavourable.

With N nodes you get up to N(N-1) ordered links (or N receive viewpoints of a common illuminator). Each link is a different projection.

       N=1                        N=4
   TX ────► RX          N1 ◄──────────► N2
                         ▲ ╲          ╱ ▲
   one projection        │  ╲   ▣    ╱  │      12 ordered links
   many blind spots      │   ╲      ╱   │      → geometric diversity
                         ▼    ╲    ╱    ▼      → blind spots of one link
                        N4 ◄───────────► N3      are covered by another

Three distinct benefits, and it is worth keeping them separate:

  1. Coverage. A person invisible to node 1's geometry is visible to node 3's.
  2. Disambiguation. Intersecting constraints from several links localise a perturbation that a single link can only detect, not place.
  3. Spoof resistance. A real body perturbs every link that passes through its location, in a geometrically consistent way. An injected fake signal on one link cannot satisfy the constraints of the others. This is precisely the check in ruvsense/adversarial.rs. [CODE]

The repo's placement study finds 100% coverage of a 1–4 occupant household at N = 5 anchors [SYNTH] — which is the stated reason the reference deployment is six nodes: you are at the plateau, with one spare.

2.12 Bandwidth is resolution — and why channel hopping exists

Radar resolution in range is set by bandwidth, not by frequency:

Physics

Range resolution

Δ R = c2B

  • 20 MHz (one WiFi channel): Δ R = 3 × 1082 × 2 × 107 = 7.5 m
  • 60 MHz (channels 1 + 6 + 11 stitched): Δ R = 2.5 m
  • 160 MHz (802.11ax wide channel): Δ R = 0.94 m
  • 4 GHz (a 60 GHz mmWave radar): Δ R = 3.75 cm

Read that first line again. With a single 20 MHz WiFi channel you cannot resolve two reflectors closer together than 7.5 metres. You are not building an imaging radar; you are building a perturbation detector with essentially no native range resolution.

This one number explains an enormous amount of this project's architecture:

  • Why channel hopping exists. Hopping 1/6/11 and stitching the bands (after correcting the per-channel oscillator phase offset) synthesises 60 MHz of aperture — 3× the range resolution. That is ruvsense/multiband.rs + ruvsense/phase_align.rs, specified in ADR-029 §2.3. [CODE]
  • Why sparse recovery is used for CIR. With only 52–64 frequency samples you cannot simply FFT your way to a clean impulse response. You must exploit the fact that the true CIR is sparse (few dominant reflectors) and solve an underdetermined inverse problem with an ℓ1 penalty. That is ISTA in ruvsense/cir.rs, per ADR-134. [CODE]
  • Why the system claims "presence and vitals", not "imaging". Anyone claiming centimetre-accurate skeletal imaging from a single 20 MHz WiFi link is claiming something the bandwidth does not support. This repository is careful about that; see Part 6.

2.13 Doppler and the Body Velocity Profile

This is the core insight of the Widar3.0 line of work, and it is why BVP is a standard intermediate representation in the field.

Part 3 — From signal to inference

Physics gives you a matrix of complex numbers. Applications want a sentence: "someone is in the bedroom, breathing normally." This part is the bridge.

3.1 The central difficulty: domain shift

Before any technique, understand the problem that defines this field.

Note

A model trained on CSI from one room does not work in another room.

Not "works a bit worse" — often near chance. The reasons are structural:

  • The multipath structure is a fingerprint of the room, not the person. Move the furniture and the fingerprint changes.
  • The transmitter and receiver positions set the whole geometry. A model can trivially learn "subcarrier 23 goes up when someone sits" — a fact true only in the room it was trained in. This failure has a name in the literature: coordinate overfitting, diagnosed by PerceptAlign and cited directly in calibration/geometry.rs. [CODE]
  • Chipsets differ in subcarrier count, scaling, and phase artefacts.
  • Even the same room drifts: temperature, humidity, an open door, a new router.

There are four broad strategies, and it is worth knowing which one any given system has picked, because it determines everything else about its architecture:

StrategyIdeaCostWho uses it
Domain-invariant featuresTransform to a representation the room cannot see — BVP, Doppler spectra, velocityLoses fine detailWidar3.0 line; bvp.rs here
Massive diverse trainingTrain on enough rooms that the model generalisesNeeds a huge, expensive datasetLarge-model WiFi-pose research
Per-room calibrationAccept the room-specificity; learn each room quickly and cheaplyRequires an enrollment ritual per installRuView's primary bet — ADR-151
Adaptation / transferPretrain a general encoder, fine-tune per room with a small adapterBest of both, most machineryRuView's stated upgrade path (LoRA over a frozen RF encoder)

RuView's architectural thesis, stated plainly: specialisation over scale. Rather than one large model that must generalise across all rooms, fit a bank of tiny models to your room in a few minutes, and be honest when the room drifts far enough that the bank is stale. This is why the calibration crate is pure Rust with no neural framework at all — the specialists are thresholds, prototype vectors, and band-limited periodicity reads. [CODE]

Two statistical tools appear repeatedly and are worth understanding on their own:

Welford's online algorithm computes running mean and variance in one pass with no accumulated round-off, without storing the samples. That property — constant memory, numerically stable — is what makes it viable on the microcontroller as well as on the host. It is used for the empty-room baseline, for longitudinal drift tracking, and inside the firmware's Tier 1 DSP.

Von Mises statistics are the circular analogue of the Gaussian. Phase lives on a circle: the mean of 359° and 1° is 0°, not 180°. Any code that averages phase linearly is wrong, subtly and intermittently. The baseline calibration uses circular statistics precisely to avoid this.

3.4 Layer 3 — Estimation: rate extraction

For breathing and heart rate the task is: given a noisy 1-D series, find the dominant periodicity in a known band.

Three methods, in increasing robustness:

  1. Zero-crossing counting after bandpass filtering. Cheap enough for a microcontroller (the firmware uses a biquad IIR bandpass plus zero crossings). Fragile: a single noise-induced crossing inflates the rate.
  2. Autocorrelation peak. Correlate the signal with itself at increasing lags; the first strong peak inside the band is the period. Far more robust to additive noise, because noise decorrelates and the periodic component does not. This is what fixed the heart-rate estimator in this project (issue #987), and it is what calibration/extract.rs uses. [CODE]
  3. Spectral peak with inter-subcarrier coherence weighting. Compute the spectrum on many subcarriers and weight each by how consistent its phase is with the others. A real physiological rhythm is coherent across subcarriers; noise is not. Used in vitals/heartrate.rs. [CODE]
Note

A trap worth naming. Breathing harmonics land inside the heart band. A 20 BPM respiration (0.33 Hz) has a third harmonic at 1.0 Hz = 60 BPM — squarely in the middle of a plausible heart rate. A naive heart-rate estimator will confidently report your breathing's third harmonic as your pulse. This is why the test plan's heart-rate protocol has you hold your breath: it removes the interferer so you can tell whether you are measuring a heart or an artefact.

3.7 Layer 6 — Tracking: persistence over time

Instantaneous detections flicker. Tracking imposes temporal consistency:

  • Kalman filtering with a constant-velocity model. Each tracked keypoint carries a 6-D state (x,y,z,vx,vy,vz); the filter predicts forward and corrects on measurement. ruvsense/pose_tracker.rs
  • Track lifecycle — Tentative → Active → Lost → Terminated — so a one-frame blip never becomes a reported person.
  • Data association by a joint cost: 60% Mahalanobis distance (is this measurement geometrically plausible for this track?) + 40% embedding cosine similarity (does it look like the same person?). The embedding term is what prevents identity swaps when two people cross.
  • Coherence gating — ruvsense/coherence_gate.rs — which decides per frame whether to Accept the measurement, run PredictOnly with inflated noise, Reject it, or trigger Recalibrate after 10 s of sustained low coherence. This is how the system avoids slowly absorbing environmental drift into its model of a person. [CODE]

An unusual and, frankly, admirable feature of this project is that it treats "can this claim be checked" as a first-class engineering requirement.

  • The capability matrix: docs/WITNESS-LOG-028.md — a 33-row table of claims with per-claim evidence, distinguishing empirical from claimed.
  • Evidence levels: ruview-unified (ADR-273..282) explicitly labels its numbers L0 — synthetic until real-data validation.

Six nodes transmitting continuously on one channel interfere with each other and with the WiFi you are trying to measure. The failure mode you must understand before touching hardware

Note

The display false-positive → 0 pps CSI starvation. [FIELD]

The firmware supports an optional SH8601 QSPI AMOLED panel. That panel is write-only — there is no readback path — so display_hal_init_panel() "succeeds" on a board with no panel attached. When the firmware believes it has a display, it keeps the promiscuous filter at MGMT-only and never calls csi_collector_enable_data_capture().

The result: CSI yield collapses to 0 packets/second while the node associates, pings, and looks perfectly healthy on the network. The current source recovers by additionally requiring FT3168 touch readback (main/display_task.c:121), but the reliable fix is to compile display support out entirely (# CONFIG_DISPLAY_ENABLE is not set), making has_display a compile-time false.

This is the single nastiest failure in the project, it wastes hours, and it is why the test plan builds firmware from source rather than using the prebuilt binaries (which predate the fix).

Design decisions worth noting:

  • 8-bit I/Q, not floats. Quantisation is acceptable because the useful signal is a change, not an absolute level, and it keeps a node's stream at ~5 KB/s.
  • Sequence numbers. Let the host detect loss without a reliability layer.
  • UDP, not TCP. A late CSI frame is worthless; retransmission would add latency and jitter for no benefit. Loss is preferable to delay.
  • Per-frame node ID. Makes the aggregator stateless with respect to source addresses — which is exactly what makes the Docker-on-Windows relay workaround possible.
  • RSSI and noise floor in-band. Lets the host compute SNR per frame and gate on link quality without a side channel.

Per-subcarrier amplitude mean/variance (Welford) and circular phase statistics of the empty room. Everything downstream is expressed as a deviation from this, which is what makes stationary hardware artefacts cancel.

Guard rail: an abort on running z-score > threshold (default 2.0). If something moves during calibration it refuses to bake a person into the "empty" model rather than silently producing a poisoned baseline. [CODE]

Stage 2 — enrollment

Eight guided anchors, each a short labelled capture:

#AnchorDurationTeaches
1empty15 sThe negative class
2stand_still15 sPresence without motion
3sit15 sPosture: sitting
4lie_down15 sPosture: lying
5breathe_slow30 sLow-rate respiration (needs the window — see §2.8)
6breathe_normal30 sNormal respiration
7small_move15 sRestlessness
8sleep_posture30 sThe overnight-monitoring case

The adaptive quality gate is where the real engineering is. Bad anchors poison small models far more than large ones, so an anchor is only accepted if its statistics match what it is supposed to teach.

Note

A subtle bug this crate documents and avoids (ADR-152, "z-band squeeze"): motion is not taken from a threshold on the deviation z-score. That flag fires on amplitude_z_median > 2.0 — deviation from the empty baseline — which conflates presence strength with motion. A strongly-reflecting person standing perfectly still would score z > 2 on every frame and be rejected as "too much motion". Instead, motion is derived from the frame-to-frame change in the deviation series (|Δz|, |Δφ|), which is presence-independent. [CODE]

This is a good example of the kind of error that is invisible in a demo and fatal in deployment.

The session is event-sourced, so re-enrollment is incremental and auditable.

Stage 2b — geometry (ADR-152 §2.1.1–2.1.2)

Optional records of where each node physically is: position (m), orientation (rad), and a free-form method provenance string ("tape-measure", "floor-plan", "unknown").

Why bother: PerceptAlign diagnosed coordinate overfitting — heads trained without an explicit layout model memorise the deployment geometry and break elsewhere. The cheap half of the fix is simply to record the geometry so every bank knows what layout it was trained under.

geometry_embedding.rs turns that record into a fixed 32-dim conditioning vector with three deliberate properties: fixed dimension for any node count, permutation-invariant (sorted by node ID, order-free aggregates), and total — an all-unknown layout yields a well-defined vector, never NaN. Adversarial inputs (non-finite coordinates, absurd magnitudes) are treated as unmeasured rather than crashing. It is explicitly not a trained network; the learned part would live in whatever head consumes it. [CODE]

Stage 4 — the specialist bank

Six small models, one per biological signal. Each is a threshold, a handful of nearest-prototype vectors, or a band-limited periodicity read:

SpecialistOutput
PresenceSpecialistpresent / absent
PostureSpecialiststanding / sitting / lying
BreathingSpecialistBPM + confidence
HeartbeatSpecialistBPM + confidence
RestlessnessSpecialistscalar 0..1
AnomalySpecialistveto flag

The runtime fusion rules keep it honest (runtime.rs):

  • The anomaly specialist holds a veto — a physically implausible window suppresses positive vitals/posture rather than propagating a hallucination.
  • presence = absent short-circuits breathing/heartbeat/posture to None. You cannot have a respiration rate in an empty room. Systems that report one are reporting noise.
  • A STALE bank (baseline drifted) flags every reading rather than emitting confident-but-wrong values — the calibration analogue of the firmware's DEGRADED flag.

Those three rules are the difference between a system that fails loudly and one that fails silently. Prefer the former.

4.6 Vital signs — wifi-densepose-vitals

ADR-021. Four stages:

  1. preprocessor.rs — EMA-based static-component suppression → per-subcarrier residuals.
  2. breathing.rs — 0.1–0.5 Hz bandpass + zero-crossing.
  3. heartrate.rs — 0.8–2.0 Hz bandpass + autocorrelation peak + inter-subcarrier phase-coherence weighting.
  4. anomaly.rs — z-score against Welford running statistics for apnea / tachycardia / bradycardia flags.

Results land in a VitalSignStore with configurable retention.

Note there are two vitals paths: this host-side one and the firmware's Tier 2 on-device one. /api/v1/vital-signs returns the host estimate; /api/v1/edge-vitals returns the node's. Comparing them is a genuinely useful cross-check — agreement is evidence, disagreement localises the fault.

Part 5 — Life of a frame

Follow one measurement from photons to a sentence. This is the mental model to hold while debugging, because every failure in this system is a break in this chain, and knowing the chain tells you where to look.

 ① PHYSICS         An 802.11n packet crosses the room. It reflects off walls,
                   furniture, and a person's chest, which has moved 8 mm since
                   the last packet.

 ② RADIO           The ESP32-S3 receives it. To equalise it, the PHY estimates
                   the channel at each of 64 subcarrier bins. That estimate is
                   handed to firmware via the CSI callback, in radio context.
                        ↓  ~200–500 callbacks/s
 ③ RATE LIMIT      csi_collector rate-limits to ~50 Hz and writes into an SPSC
                   ring buffer. Never blocks. Overflow drops, silently.
                        ↓
 ④ TIER            Core 1 drains the ring. Tier 0 → passthrough. Tier 1 →
                   unwrap/Welford/top-K. Tier 2 → also runs vitals, presence,
                   fall on-device and emits a 32-byte packet at 1 Hz.
                        ↓
 ⑤ SERIALISE       20-byte ADR-018 header + i8 I/Q pairs.  magic 0xC5110001
                        ↓
 ⑥ TRANSPORT       UDP → target_ip:5005. No retransmit. ENOMEM backoff 100 ms
                   if lwIP runs out of packet buffers.
                        ↓   ~124 bytes × 20 Hz × N nodes
 ⑦ INGEST          sensing-server binds :5005. Esp32CsiParser validates magic,
                   bounds-checks n_subcarriers, decodes I/Q → complex.
                        ↓
 ⑧ NORMALISE       Hampel → phase sanitize (unwrap + detrend) → hardware_norm
                   resample to canonical 56 bins → z-score.
                        ↓
 ⑨ REFERENCE       Subtract the ADR-135 empty-room baseline. Output is now a
                   *deviation*, so stationary hardware artefacts cancel.
                        ↓
 ⑩ REPRESENT       Depending on the consumer: per-subcarrier statistics, a
                   top-K selection, an STFT spectrogram, a BVP, a sparse CIR,
                   or a 128-dim embedding.
                        ↓
 ⑪ FUSE            Multiple nodes/channels: multiband stitch → phase_align →
                   attention-weighted multistatic fusion → coherence gate
                   decides Accept / PredictOnly / Reject / Recalibrate.
                        ↓
 ⑫ INFER           The room's specialist bank (or vitals pipeline, or tracker)
                   emits {value, confidence} per quantity. Anomaly may veto.
                   Absent presence short-circuits vitals to None.
                        ↓
 ⑬ TRACK           Kalman update; track lifecycle; re-ID by embedding.
                        ↓
 ⑭ QUALITY         QualityScore attaches evidence refs and contradiction flags.
                   Contradictions lower the downstream privacy class.
                        ↓
 ⑮ SEMANTICS       "someone-sleeping", "bed-exit", "possible-distress".
                        ↓
 ⑯ EGRESS          REST / WebSocket / MQTT / Matter / HAP — subject to the
                   privacy gate: P0 raw and P3–P5 identity never leave the box.

Where the chain breaks, and the symptom

BreakSymptom you observeFirst thing to check
② no CSI enabledNode online, 0 ppsDisplay false-positive → rebuild with CONFIG_DISPLAY_ENABLE unset
② no traffic to measureLow but non-zero ppsCSI only exists when packets exist. Generate traffic.
③ ring overflowGaps in sequence numbersNode is CPU-starved; lower tier or subcarrier count

Debugging heuristic: walk the chain forward from the physics, not backward from the symptom. The two most common failures (0 pps CSI, and firewall-blocked UDP) both occur before any interesting code runs, and both present as "the sophisticated part is broken."

Part 10 — Glossary

TermMeaning
ADRArchitecture Decision Record. A numbered document capturing one design decision, its context, and its consequences. This repo has 207 of them and the code cites them by number.
AGCAutomatic Gain Control. The receiver's per-packet gain adjustment — a source of amplitude discontinuities in CSI.
AnchorA short, labelled capture during enrollment (empty, sit, breathe_slow, …) used to fit the room's specialists.
Bank (specialist bank)The versioned collection of small models fitted to one room, tied to the baseline it was trained against.
BaselineThe empty-room statistical fingerprint (Welford amplitude + circular phase). Everything downstream is a deviation from it.
BFI / BFLDBeamforming Feedback Information — compressed channel state that 802.11ac/ax stations report to the AP. BFLD is this project's privacy-gated sensing layer built on it.
Bistatic / MultistaticBistatic: transmitter and receiver in different places. Multistatic: many such pairs, giving geometric diversity.
BVPBody Velocity Profile — a velocity × time representation that generalises across environments better than raw CSI.
CFO / SFO / PDDCarrier Frequency Offset / Sampling Frequency Offset / Packet Detection Delay. The three hardware artefacts that make raw CSI phase unusable.
CIRChannel Impulse Response — the time-domain view: what the room does to an instantaneous pulse. Inverse-FFT dual of the CFR.
CogThis project's packaging format for an edge module — a signed, versioned binary that runs on a node or gateway.
Coherence gateThe rule that decides per frame whether to Accept / PredictOnly / Reject / Recalibrate, based on how consistent the observation is with a running template.
COCO keypointsThe 17-joint skeleton convention (nose, eyes, ears, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles).
CSIChannel State Information — the per-subcarrier complex channel estimate. The measurement this whole system is built on.
DensePoseOriginally a computer-vision task: dense body-surface correspondence from an image. Here, the aspiration of doing something similar from RF.
Edge tierThe firmware's 0–3 setting controlling how much DSP happens on the node vs. the host. Runtime-switchable via NVS.
EnrollmentThe guided capture of labelled anchors that teaches the system your room.
Fresnel zoneNested ellipsoids between TX and RX defined by path-length difference. Determines whether a given chest position produces a strong or null breathing signal.
HNSWHierarchical Navigable Small World — the graph structure enabling sub-linear approximate nearest-neighbour search over embeddings.
HT20 / HT40 / HE20802.11n 20 / 40 MHz and 802.11ax 20 MHz PHY modes. Determines subcarrier count; must match the --tier flag in the CLI.
I/QIn-phase and Quadrature — the real and imaginary parts of a complex sample. ADR-018 ships them as signed 8-bit pairs.
MATMass Casualty Assessment Tool — the disaster-response vertical for finding survivors in rubble.
Min-cutThe graph partition of minimum total edge weight. Used here to separate multiple people and to detect adversarial injection.
MultipathThe many reflected paths a signal takes. The reason CSI is informative and the reason it is hard.
NDPNull Data Packet — a frame with no payload, transmitted purely so the receiver can measure the channel.
NVSNon-Volatile Storage — the ESP32 key-value partition holding runtime config. Changing it never requires reflashing the app.
OFDMOrthogonal Frequency-Division Multiplexing — parallel narrow subcarriers. The reason CSI is a vector, not a scalar.
PCK@20Percentage of Correct Keypoints within a 20-pixel (or normalised) threshold. The pose accuracy metric used here.
Privacy class (P0–P5)BFLD's egress classification. P0 raw and P3–P5 identity/biometric are structurally forbidden from leaving the device.
RSSIReceived Signal Strength Indicator — one scalar per packet. Far less informative than CSI; the fallback for laptops.
RuvSenseThis project's multistatic sensing bounded context (ADR-029/030) inside wifi-densepose-signal.
RVFThe RuVector model container format. Binary RVF is what --model parses; the Hugging Face bundle currently ships JSONL RVF.
SpecialistOne small room-calibrated model for one biological signal (presence, posture, breathing, heartbeat, restlessness, anomaly).
STALEThe bank's honest degradation flag: the baseline has drifted far enough that readings should not be trusted.
SubcarrierOne of the parallel narrow frequency slots in an OFDM symbol. 312.5 kHz apart in 802.11n.
TDMTime-Division Multiplexing — giving each node its own transmit slot so six nodes do not jam each other.
TomographyReconstructing a spatial occupancy map by inverting many path-integral (per-link attenuation) measurements.
Welford's algorithmNumerically stable, constant-memory online mean and variance. Used on both the MCU and the host.
Witness chainAn Ed25519-signed audit trail proving a reported measurement is the one actually produced.

Closing note

The best thing about this repository is not any single capability. It is that it tells you which of its capabilities are real. That is rare in this field, where the gap between a demo and a deployment is enormous and rarely disclosed.

If you take three things from this document:

  1. Placement and calibration dominate. More than the model, more than the node count, more than anything in the software. §2.10, §3.1.
  2. Bandwidth is resolution. 20 MHz gives you 7.5 m. Every architectural choice in this project is downstream of that number. §2.12.
  3. State how you measured it. A characterised 70% is worth more than an unreproducible 95%. Part 6.

Then go run plans/ruview-esp32s3-test-plan.md.
